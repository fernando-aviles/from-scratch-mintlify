Outages happen. Relaybox is designed so that a few hours of downtime on your side doesn't lose a single event.

## During a short outage

For anything under {maxRetryWindow}, you don't have to do anything. Relaybox [retries automatically](/concepts/delivery/retries) and delivers every event once your endpoint is back.

<Tip>
  If you know an outage is coming — a database migration, say — [pause the endpoint](/concepts/endpoints#endpoint-states) first. Events queue up and are delivered in order when you resume, without burning through retry attempts.
</Tip>

## After a long outage

If your endpoint was down longer than the retry window, some events will be marked **failed**. Replay them once you've recovered:

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Dashboard">
    1. Open the endpoint and select the **Failed** filter.
    2. Choose the time range of the outage.
    3. Click **Replay all**. Relaybox shows how many events will be sent and asks you to confirm.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="CLI">
    ```bash
    relaybox events replay \
      --endpoint ep_5Np8Wz \
      --status failed \
      --since "2026-10-18T02:00:00Z" \
      --until "2026-10-18T09:30:00Z"
    ```
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="API">
    See [Replay events](/api-reference/events/replay).
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Get alerted

Don't find out about failures from your customers. Under **Settings → Alerts**, choose when to be notified:

| Alert | Triggers when |
| --- | --- |
| **Failure rate** | More than a chosen percentage of attempts fail over 5 minutes |
| **Backlog** | An endpoint's oldest pending event is older than a chosen duration |
| **Endpoint disabled** | Relaybox disables an endpoint after 5 days of failures |

Alerts can go to email, Slack, PagerDuty, or any webhook URL — including a Relaybox endpoint.

<Warning>
  Don't route alerts *only* to an endpoint that depends on the same infrastructure as the one you're monitoring. If that infrastructure goes down, you won't hear about it.
</Warning>
